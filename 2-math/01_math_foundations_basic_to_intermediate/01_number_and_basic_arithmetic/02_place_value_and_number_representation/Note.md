# Place Value and Number Representation

# Part 1: Understanding Place Value

Place value means the value of a digit depends on its position in a number.

Each place to the left is 10 times bigger than the place to its right.

Like: 4,582 has 4 thousands, 5 hundreds, 8 tens, and 2 ones.

---
## 1 Place-Value Chart

A place-value chart helps you identify the position and value of each digit.

````
Thousands	Hundreds	Tens	Ones
  3	           6	     2	     7
This chart represents 3,627
- The digit 3 has a value of 3,000.
- The digit 6 has a value of 600.
- The digit 2 has a value of 20.
- The digit 7 has a value of 7.
````
---

## 2 Face Value and Place Value

Face value is the digit itself, regardless of its position.

Place value is the value of the digit based on its position.
```
For examples:  
Face Value:
- In 6,248, the face value of 6 is always 6.
Place value:
- In 6,248, the 6 is in the thousands place, so its place value is 6,000.
- In 2,468, the 6 is in the tens place, so its place value is 60.
- In 1,236, the 6 is in the ones place, so its place value is 6
```
Quick trick: Face value never changes, but place value can change!

---
## 3 The Role of Zero in Place Value

Zero is used as a placeholder when a place has no units of that value.
```
For example, in 4,052:
Thousands	Hundreds	Tens	Ones
   4	       0	      5	      2 
- 4 = 4,000
- 0 = No hundreds
- 5 = 50[..](..)
- 2 = 2

Expanded form:
(4,052 = 4,000 + 50 + 2)

What should you remember?
- Zero means there are none in that place.
- Zero helps keep the digits in the correct positions.
- Without zero, 4,052 could become 452, which is a different number.
```
Easy tip: Zero may have no value in its own place, but it helps the whole number keep its correct value!

---
## 4 Expanded Forms 

```
For example:

The number 345 in expanded form is:

300 + 40 + 5
- 3 means 300 (hundreds).
- 4 means 40 (tens).
- 5 means 5 (ones).
```
Means breaking a number into smaller parts to show the value of each digit.

---
## 5 Standard Form and Word Form

Numbers can be represented in three common ways.

```
Standard form: The number written using digits — 6,043
Word form: The number written in words — Six thousand forty-three
Expanded form: The sum of its place values — (6,000 + 40 + 3)
```
Be careful with zero. In 6,043, there are 6 thousands, no hundreds, 4 tens, and 3 ones.

# Part 2: Comparing and Ordering Numbers

Comparing numbers means finding which number is greater, smaller, or equal.

Ordering numbers means arranging them from smallest to largest or largest to smallest.

- Greater than (>): The number is bigger. Example: 8 > 5
- Less than (<): The number is smaller. Example: 3 < 7
- Equal to (=): Both numbers have the same value. Example: 4 = 4
- Different number of digits: The number with more digits is bigger. Example: 1,000 > 999
- Same number of digits: Compare from left to right until the first different digit. Example: 456 > 423
- Ascending order: Arrange from smallest to largest. Example: 2, 4, 6, 8
- Descending order: Arrange from largest to smallest. Example: 8, 6, 4, 2
- Number line: Numbers on the right are bigger; numbers on the left are smaller.
- Zeroes and digit positions: A digit's position affects its value. Example: 500 > 50
---
# Parts 3: Rounding Numbers

## 1 What Is Rounding?

Rounding means changing a number to a nearby number that is easier to use.

---
## 2 Rounding Rules
- Find the place value you need to round to.
- Look at the digit immediately to its right.
- If the digit is 0–4, keep the rounding digit the same.
- If the digit is 5–9, increase the rounding digit by 1.
- Change all digits to the right to zeroes.
---

## 3 Rounding to Different Place Values

Nearest ten: Look at the ones digit.

Nearest hundred: Look at the tens digit.

Nearest thousand: Look at the hundreds digit.

Nearest ten thousand: Look at the thousands digit.

```
Examples:

Nearest ten: 63 = 60, 68 = 70
Nearest hundred: 342 = 300, 378 = 400
Nearest thousand: 4,326 = 4,000, 4,726 = 5,000
Nearest ten thousand: 67,428 = 70,000, 62,428 = 60,000

```
# Summary
- Place value depends on a digit's position in a base-ten number.
- Each position to the left is worth 10 times the position to its right.
- Face value is the digit itself; place value is the digit's value in its position.
- Zero acts as a placeholder when a place has no units.
- Expanded form expresses a number as the sum of its place values.
- Standard form uses digits; word form uses words.
- When comparing positive whole numbers with different digit counts, the number with more digits is greater.
- When digit counts are equal, compare from left to right until the first difference.
- Ascending order means smallest to largest; descending order means largest to smallest.
- To round, examine the digit immediately to the right of the requested place.
- A deciding digit of 0–4 means round down; 5–9 means round up.
- After rounding, replace every digit to the right of the rounding place with zero.
- For nearest ten, inspect the ones digit.
- For nearest hundred, inspect the tens digit.
- For nearest thousand, inspect the hundreds digit.
- For nearest ten thousand, inspect the thousands digit.
- Estimation uses rounded numbers to produce an approximate result.
