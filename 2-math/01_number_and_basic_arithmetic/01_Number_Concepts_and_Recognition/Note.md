# Numbers and Basic Arithmetic
---

## 1. Counting and Number Recognition

1-Counting Numbers: 

Counting is the process of saying numbers in a specific order to determine how many objects are in,
a group. The counting sequence begins with 1 and continues as 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, and so on.

2-Number Recognition:

Number recognition means identifying a number by its written symbol, spoken name, or quantity.

Numbers can be represented in different ways.
- Numeral: the written symbol, such as 7.
- Number name: the word, such as seven.
- Quantity: the amount represented, such as seven objects.

3-Counting Forward and Backward:

Counting forward helps you find the next number. Counting backward helps you find the previous number and prepares you for subtraction.
- The number after 12 is 13.
- The number before 12 is 11.

4 Comparing Numbers:

Comparing numbers means determining which number is greater, smaller, or equal.

## 2. Whole Numbers and Natural Numbers

1-Natural Numbers:

Natural numbers are the numbers used for counting.

Like: 1,2,3,....

2-Whole Numbers:

Whole numbers are the non-negative integers, including zero.

Like: 0,1,2,3,....

Whole numbers do not include negative numbers, fractions, or decimals that are not whole-number values.

3-Integer: A number that can be positive, negative, or zero, without fractions or decimal parts.

Important: Negative whole numbers are numbers like (-1, -2, -3). We call them negative whole numbers informally,

but in standard mathematics, they are not classified as whole numbers.

In simple words:
- Whole numbers: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4...
- Negative numbers: -1, -2, -3, -4...

Negative whole numbers means -1, -2, -3, etc., but in maths, we call them negative integers, not whole numbers.

Remember: Whole numbers do not include negative numbers.

## 3. Even and Odd Numbers

1-Even Numbers:

An even number is an integer that can be divided by 2 without leaving a remainder

Like 0,2,4,6,8

```
Formula: n = 2k
```
Why do we use 2? Because every even number can be divided by 2 with no remainder.
- \(n\) = an even number
- \(2\) = means we multiply by 2
- \(k\) = an integer (a whole-number value that can be positive, negative, or zero)

Like: 14 ÷ 2 = 7 

The remainder is 0, so 14 is even.
